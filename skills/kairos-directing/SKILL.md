# Directing is deciding

Two videos can share the same idea and the same tools, and only one feels *made*. The difference isn't the tool:
in the directed one, someone decided every element on purpose. An undirected video takes the default for
everything: subject in the middle, even light, constant speed, nothing that peaks. Your job is to replace each
default with a decision.

## 1. Write the brief before any code

Five lines, agreed with the user when anything is unclear:

1. **The idea** in one sentence.
2. **Where it plays:** a vertical phone feed (9:16, sound often off, the first second decides everything) or a
   wide screen (16:9, viewers who chose to watch).
3. **The feeling** you want to leave: calm, wonder, tension, joy, curiosity...
4. **The one big moment:** the peak everything builds to.
5. **The ending:** how it lands, not just where it stops.

## 2. Make a shot list

Plan shots before drawing anything. A table is enough:

| # | Time | Shot | What we see | What moves | Words / sound |
|---|------|------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| 1 | 0.0-2.5 | wide, 24 mm | the street at night, the moon | slow push-in | "Every night..." |
| 2 | 2.5-4.5 | close-up, 85 mm | the girl's face | blink, look up | |

Rules of thumb:
- **Hook in the first 1-2 seconds:** motion plus a question or a surprise. Never open on an empty, still frame.
- **Shot length:** 2-4 s per shot for short vertical videos, longer for calm or wide-screen pieces. Vary it: a run
  of quick shots makes the next long one feel important.
- **Vary shot sizes:** wide (where are we?) → medium (who?) → close-up (how do they feel?) → insert (the detail that
  tells the story, like a clock or a letter).
- **A motif that returns:** an object, a color or a shape seen early and again at the end ties the film together.
- **Cut on motion:** change shots while something moves; it hides the cut.

## 3. The four decisions in every shot

1. **Where the light goes.** One light leads the eye to the subject; everything else sits darker. The direction of
   the light says the time of day and the mood (low and warm: evening, nostalgia; hard from above: noon, tension;
   cold from behind: night, mystery). A thin rim of light separates the subject from the background.
2. **Four layers of depth.** Foreground (something close, often out of focus or framing the shot), the subject,
   the midground, the background or sky. Flat images have one layer. When the camera moves, the layers slide at
   different speeds (parallax) and the frame comes alive.
3. **A breath before the hit.** Before the big moment, slow down: hold the frame, let the motion settle, drop the
   sound, wait 0.3-1 s. The contrast between stillness and the hit is what makes the hit land.
4. **The one big moment.** One peak per film: the biggest motion, the brightest light, the boldest color, the
   loudest sound. If everything is big, nothing is.

## 4. Rhythm and motion

- Nothing natural moves at a constant speed. Ease in and out; let things overshoot and settle.
- Stagger: when several things move, start them a few frames apart instead of all at once.
- Hold important frames long enough to read them (at least 1 s for an image, longer for text).
- End on a held frame, not mid-motion.

## 5. Composition and color

- Put the subject on a third, not dead center, unless symmetry is the point. Leave room in the direction they look
  or move.
- Vertical videos: keep faces and words out of the top ~12% and the bottom ~20% of the frame, where the phone's
  app interface sits.
- A limited palette: 3-5 colors. Keep one accent color for the thing that matters most.
- Contrast guides the eye: the brightest, sharpest, most colorful spot is where viewers look first.

## 6. The ending

Give the film an end beat: the motif returns, a reveal, a final line, or a clean title held for 1-2 s. Don't just
stop when the content runs out.

## 7. Review pass

Look at the film the way a viewer would, with `kairos_frame`:
- Frames at 0 s and 1 s: is there a hook?
- One frame per shot: can you tell at a glance where to look? Is the light leading there?
- The big moment: is it really the peak (size, light, color, motion)?
- Any stretch longer than ~3 s where nothing changes?
- The last frame: does it land?

Fix what fails, then check again.
